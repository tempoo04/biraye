    The research behind BirAye

    BirAye's design rests on two studies — one on the value of Hifz itself, one on the learning science behind its core feature.

    Nawaz, N. & Jahangir, S. F. (2015). Effects of memorizing Quran by heart (Hifz) on later academic achievement. Journal of Islamic Studies and Culture, 3(1), 58–64.

    About Found that children who completed Hifz later showed stronger academic achievement — disciplined verbatim memorization trains memory, attention, and persistence.

    In BirAye This is the whole premise: BirAye treats Hifz as serious verbatim training and gives it a retention engine, instead of being just another Quran audio player.

    Birnbaum, M. S., Kornell, N., Bjork, E. L. & Bjork, R. A. (2013). Why interleaving enhances inductive learning: The roles of discrimination and retrieval. Memory & Cognition, 41(3), 392–402.

    About Showed that studying similar items mixed together — instead of one at a time — helps the brain tell them apart, because it forces discrimination between confusable things.

    In BirAye This is the Similar (mutashabihat) tab: near-identical verses are drilled side by side with the differing words highlighted, so you stop confusing them — the #1 cause of Hifz errors.

    How BirAye works

    BirAye helps you memorize the Quran verse by verse and — more importantly — stop forgetting it. Five tabs:

    • Read — pick a surah, listen to each ayah, and tap “Memorize” to start tracking it.
    • Drill — loop a range of verses on repeat, with your own repeat counts and speed, to drum them in.
    • Similar — browse near-identical verses that are easy to mix up; differing words are highlighted so you learn the difference.
    • Review — verses come back for recall right before you’d forget them; the help fades as your memory strengthens. Rate yourself honestly.
    • Log — a record of every tracked ayah you can export and share with a teacher.
